The problem

A report said “possible bone cancer, recommend follow-up.” No one saw it.

The patient found it in his own chart a year later. A story from our clinical mentor.

  • The radiologist compares each follow-up film with the prior by hand.
  • She writes the comparison from a blank page.
  • Some image pairs cannot support a call, and nothing says so.
How it reads

Two blind reads. Then the question.

  • The reason for the scan is hidden until both reads finish. The release time is on screen.
  • Each finding shows whether the two reads agreed: 2 of 2, or 1 of 2 !
  • Agreement is consistency, not accuracy. A radiologist reads every study.
The refusal · recorded output, case refusal_nodule

Prior is PA. Today’s film is a portable AP.

Can’t determine: Has the right lower zone opacity changed in size or density since the prior?

Reason: Prior is PA and current is AP and underexposed. Projection and exposure differences can make a basal opacity look larger or denser.

Next: CT chest, or repeat PA and lateral when the patient is able.

ScanShift does not call the opacity larger, smaller or unchanged. “Can’t determine” has the same weight as a finding, with a reason and a next step.

Four agents, one boundary each
Lightbox

Reads the two images on Claude Fable 5.2. Its blind reads get images and view labels only.

Stat

Sorts the worklist from the findings and the chart. Never sees an image.

Courier

Drafts the report. Its handoff mode refuses to run before the attending signs.

Charge

Plain code. Enforces the order of steps, the locks and the audit log.

  • The model gives no measurements. The radiologist types them in.
  • Resident reviews every finding. Attending signs. Only then does the follow-up order unlock for the provider to sign.

After the signature

One signed read, a draft for each person downstream.

  • Provider message, pre-filled follow-up order, tumor board, surgeon and family page.
  • Not opened by a high-tier deadline: re-sent and escalated.
  • Why it matters: 41.3 percent of patients with a CT-detected incidental lung nodule completed recommended follow-up at two New York teaching hospitals (AuntMinnie summary. We have not read the original paper).
